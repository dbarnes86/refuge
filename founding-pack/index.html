I

At a glance

Refuge is a private estate on Bordeaux’s Right Bank, near Saint-Émilion, owned by 26 founding families and run by a full-time operator. We are raising €2.3M from those families, with a €1.0M bank loan alongside, to buy and restore a domaine of 6–10 hectares and open in 2027.

  • What it is: a working estate with a main house, 14 lodges and its own power, water and food.
  • Who it is for: families who spent the last ten years at full speed and want somewhere that is already theirs when they arrive.
  • What you hold: shares in the company that owns the land and buildings, plus the right to use the estate. You are an owner, not a guest.
  • Three forms: Estate Partner (€175,000, six places, your own lodge), Founding Partner (€90,000, ten places), Member (€35,000, ten places). Each carries an annual fee.
  • Who runs it: a full-time operator from the founding team, with an experienced estate manager. Families oversee both through an elected council.
  • When: founding commitments now, property secured in the first half of 2027, opening to families in late 2027.
II

The idea

Refuge is a self-sufficient estate where families come to disconnect: from screens, from schedules, from the speed of the last ten years. You share it with a small group of people who want the same things you do: time with their children, good food, land worth looking after, and conversation that isn’t about work.

It is not a hotel, a resort or a commune. Your lodge is private and you come and go as you like. Nobody tells you how to spend your days, and there is no programme, ideology or membership code beyond being good company. The estate is staffed, stocked and ready, whether you arrive for a long weekend or the whole of August.

Self-sufficient by design. Solar with battery storage, a borehole and water treatment, wood heat, a kitchen garden and orchard. The estate produces its own power, water and much of its food. In practice that means lower running costs, better food and a place that keeps working when the grid or the supply chain doesn’t.

III

The estate

We are buying an existing domaine of 6–10 hectares, with a house and outbuildings, within about 30 minutes of Saint-Émilion.

We buy rather than build, because French farm and natural zoning blocks most new construction. A domaine with barns we can convert is the reliable route. The search area is the edge of the Libournais, Entre-Deux-Mers and Côtes de Bourg: the Right Bank on the doorstep, at a land price that makes 6–10 hectares workable.

V

How it is held

Two French companies keep ownership and operations apart. One owns the estate, the other runs it.

  • Refuge Domaine SAS (the property company) owns the land and buildings. Founding families hold its shares in proportion to their contribution. It carries the bank loan and receives rent from the operating company.
  • Refuge Maison SAS (the operating company) employs the staff, collects annual fees and guest income, runs the estate and pays rent to the property company. The operator is its président(e).

Your shares carry your use rights. You hold a stake in a real asset, not a timeshare. The use rights are attached to the shares and pass with them.

Getting out. You can sell your shares to an incoming family approved by the estate council. Existing families have first refusal, and the estate keeps a waiting list so there is a buyer when someone wants to leave. There is no promise of a dividend or a fixed return. Any surplus goes to the maintenance reserve first.

Founders’ shares. The founders put in the work of founding and running the estate rather than a cash contribution at Estate Partner scale. They hold founder shares in the property company at a fixed percentage agreed before the first close, vesting over four years. The percentage is set out in the term sheet.

The structure will be confirmed with a French avocat and expert-comptable before any contribution is called.

VI

Who runs it

One of the founders runs Refuge full-time as operator, accountable to the families through the estate council. The other is founder and investor, and does not run the estate day to day.

From opening, the operating company employs:

  • An estate manager with hotel or private-estate experience, running the daily operation, staff and guest stays.
  • A small permanent team covering house, kitchen, grounds and systems, with seasonal help in summer.
  • A French expert-comptable for payroll, tax and both companies’ accounts.

Governance. An estate council of three is elected by the families, with an Estate Partner seat. It approves the annual budget, the operator’s pay, any contract between the estate and the founders, and every new family. Accounts are shared with every family each year. If the operator steps back, the estate manager runs the house while the council appoints a successor.

The founding team’s backgrounds and references are shared during founding conversations.

Once open

The goal is an estate that pays its own way. Annual fees cover roughly half of the estate’s yearly costs, loan included. Curated guest stays in the shared lodges, when families aren’t using them, cover the rest and fund a maintenance reserve.

If guest income falls short, the levers are a smaller team in the quiet months and a fee review at the annual meeting. Founding contributions are never used to cover running costs.

All figures are planning estimates, firmed up once a property is under offer. The full financial model is shared during founding conversations.
