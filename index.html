The idea

You built the company. You raised the round, ran the team, made the flight. The last ten years were spent at full speed, and your family felt it.

Refuge is the counterweight. An estate that is staffed, stocked and ready when you arrive, where the phones go in a drawer, the children run outside and dinner is a long table of people you’re glad to see.

Not a hotel. Not a resort. Not a commune. Your lodge is private, you come and go as you please, and nobody tells you how to spend your days.

A day at Refuge
  1. Morning

    Coffee on the terrace. Eggs from the estate’s hens, bread from the oven. Nobody has a call at nine.

  2. Midday

    The children are in the orchard or the pool. You’re on a bike between vineyards, or asleep in the shade.

  3. Evening

    One long table, food from the garden, wine from down the road. Conversation that isn’t about work.

  4. Night

    No traffic, no glow on the horizon. The estate runs on its own power and water while you sleep.

The estate
R
Setting
A domaine of 6–10 hectares on Bordeaux’s Right Bank, within half an hour of Saint-Émilion.
Self-sufficient
Solar and storage, its own borehole, a kitchen garden and orchard. The estate makes its own power, water and much of its food.
Lodges
A main house with fourteen lodges: six private, eight shared. Restored outbuildings and low-impact new builds.
Owned
Founding families own the estate itself, through shares that carry the right to use it. Owners, not guests.
Founding memberships

Twenty-six founding families, in three forms. Each carries a one-off founding contribution and an annual fee for staff and upkeep.

  1. I Estate Partner Six places · your own lodge, designed with you · €18,000 a year €175,000
  2. II Founding Partner Ten places · 28 nights a year in the shared lodges · €13,000 a year €90,000
  3. III Member Ten places · 14 nights a year in the shared lodges · €10,000 a year €35,000

No money moves until a property is under offer and the founding group is formed. The founding pack sets out the numbers, the structure and the risks in full.

Questions
Is this a timeshare?

No. Founding families hold shares in the company that owns the estate, and the right to use it comes with those shares. You can sell them to an incoming family when you choose.

Do we have to live there, or join in?

No. Most families come for long weekends and a few weeks in summer. There is no programme and no rules about how you spend your time, beyond being good company.

What happens to our money before the estate is bought?

Nothing moves until a property is under offer and the founding group is formed. The first payment then sits in escrow, and comes back in full if the group doesn’t close.

Who decides who joins?

Every new family meets Caroline and Dan and spends a day on the estate. The families’ council approves each one.
